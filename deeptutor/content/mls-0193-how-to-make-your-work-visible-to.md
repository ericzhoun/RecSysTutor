reliability</p></li><li><p>You are easy to work with, always willing to lend an helping hand to everyone around you</p></li><li><p>You come up with new ideas and create opportunities for your team</p></li></ul><p>Yet, somehow, you don’t get the recognition you deserve you should get.</p><p>And you chalk it up to “big corp politics”.</p><p>The things you tell youself:</p><ul><li><p>“I don’t play this game”</p></li><li><p>“My work speaks for itself”</p></li><li><p>“Good work should get recognized. Not my problem”</p></li></ul><p>I used to think like that and let me tell you: this “holier than you” mentality will stall your career FAST.</p><p>The problem with this mentality is mainly one: your ego.</p><p>And I have a big one! So I know what I am talking about ;)</p><p>Let’s see how I shifted things around!</p><h1>You are not as important as you think you are (sorry)</h1><div class="paywall-jump" data-component-name="PaywallToDOM"></div><p>Yeah, that’s the cold reality of things.</p><p>Your skip is not aware what exactly you are working on. </p><p>Sure, maybe at a surface level.</p><p>But not the complexity of it.</p><p>Not the roadblocks you had to overcome.</p><p>Maybe not even the impact.</p><p>And a million of other things.</p><p>Put yourself in their shoes, they care about much more high level problems.</p><p>Once you internalize this, you can then think of a solution.</p><p>There are a few things that I believe are important.</p><h2><strong>Sharing your accomplishments the right way</strong></h2><p>Share wins, small and big. That does not mean to spam your team chat every time you submit a PR.</p><p>But if you are genuine, people will care about your great work. So don’t be shy. Some examples:</p><ol><li><p>Just landed LaunchNumber10. X% impact. I believe it can help your workflow as well because reason A, B and C. Let me know what you think!</p></li><li><p>Rolling out LaunchNumber15. Heads up to oncall: i expect metrics X and Y to shift. If metric Z moves, please ping me. I will online in the evening to double check the launch.</p></li><li><p>I have just landed this new shiny thing in prod. It’s not used anywhere yet, but I plan on doing X and Y for reason Z. If you have more ideas on how to use new shiny thing, let me know: happy to experiment with other use cases!</p></li></ol><p>See what I did there?</p><p>No fluff corporate texts:</p><ul><li><p>Genuine updates</p></li><li><p>Giving heads up</p></li><li><p>Offering a collaboration opportunity to others</p></li></ul><p>Now, this is good, but you can do even better:</p><ul><li><p>Ping that Senior Lead with your launch numbers, a nice TLDR of what’s happening and future plans. Keep it short and to the point.</p></li><li><p>Book those blocks with your director to ask questions, drop what you are working and get real-time feedback on your projects / upcoming launches</p></li></ul><h2><strong>Making your manager’s life easier</strong></h2><p>Your manager is being pulled in ten directions. Performance reviews, roadmap planning, headcount battles, skip-level syncs.</p><p>The more you can make their job easier, the more visible you become.</p><p>Concrete ways to do this:</p><ul><li><p>Come to your 1:1 with a clear agenda. Don’t make them ask “so what are you working on?”</p></li><li><p>Send a short weekly update. 3 bullets max. What shipped, what’s blocked, what’s next.</p></li><li><p>When you escalate a problem, come with a proposed solution. Don’t just drop a grenade.</p></li></ul><p>This is being a reliable signal in a noisy environment.</p><h2><strong>Make visibility a habit, not a campaign</strong></h2><p>Most people stay invisible for months, then suddenly realize they need to “build their brand” before promo season.</p><p>So they start overcommunicating. Posting in every channel. Suddenly very opinionated in meetings.</p><p>The fix is simple: make it consistent and low-key.</p><ul><li><p>One team update per week. Short.</p></li><li><p>One skip-level touchpoint per month. Casual.</p></li><li><p>One cross-team collaboration surfaced per quarter. Real.</p></li></ul><p>That’s it.</p><p>You are not “playing politics” when you share your work.</p><p>You are doing your job completely.</p><p>Shipping is 80% of the work. The other 20% is making sure the right people understand what you shipped and why it matters.</p><p>If you skip that 20%, you are leaving impact on the table. Not just career impact — actual product impact. Because work that isn’t understood doesn’t get funded, extended, or built upon.</p><p>So the next time you catch yourself thinking “my work speaks for itself” — ask yourself: <em>to whom, exactly?</em></p><p>Good work deserves to be understood. Help people understand it.</p><p>That’s not ego!</p>
