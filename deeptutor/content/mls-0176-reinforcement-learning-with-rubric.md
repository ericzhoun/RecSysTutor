# Beyond Verifiable Rewards: The Rubicon Framework

Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) has proven to be a powerful technique for enhancing the capabilities of Large Language Models (LLMs), especially in domains where a definitive, checkable answer exists.

This is particularly effective for tasks like code generation, where unit tests can verify correctness, and mathematical reasoning, where the final answer can be programmatically checked.

However, this reliance on unambiguous, verifiable signals has largely confined RLVR to a narrow set of problems, leaving a vast landscape of open-ended and subjective tasks—such as creative writing, nuanced conversation, and complex humanities analysis—unaddressed.

The “Reinforcement Learning with Rubric Anchors” (Rubicon) framework was developed to bridge this gap.

Instead of a binary “correct/incorrect” reward, Rubicon utilizes a comprehensive system of detailed, multi-dimensional rubrics to provide fine-grained feedback.

These rubrics serve as structured, interpretable criteria that allow for the automatic scoring of tasks with subjective or multi-faceted outputs. The research team in [1] has built a massive bank of over 10,000 rubrics, which were generated through various methods: by human experts, by different LLMs, or through a hybrid human-LLM collaborative process.
This extensive rubric system allows the reward model to capture nuanced aspects of a response, such as stylistic tone, emotional expressiveness, and logical coherence, moving far beyond simple, verifiable correctness.

# A Two-Stage Approach to Mitigate the “Seesaw Effect”

A significant challenge in multi-task learning is the “seesaw effect,” where training a model to improve on one type of task can cause its performance on another, differing task to degrade.
The paper’s authors observed this phenomenon directly: training a model exclusively on instruction-following rubrics improved its ability to adhere to constraints but diminished its creativity. Conversely, focusing only on creativity and empathy rubrics enhanced open-ended generation at the cost of instruction-following precision.

To address this conflict, the Rubicon framework implements a strategic two-stage reinforcement learning schedule.

  * **Stage 1: Foundational Skills.** The first phase concentrates on building a robust foundation in reliable instruction-following. This stage uses programmatically verifiable checks and static, multi-dimensional rubrics to instill a strong capability for handling constraints.

  * **Stage 2: Advanced Capabilities.** Once the model has a solid base, the second phase targets more open-ended, creative, and socially-grounded tasks. This stage employs high-quality reference examples and instance-specific rubrics, often generated by more powerful agentic workflows, to cultivate adaptability and richer, more nuanced expression.

This sequential approach allows the model to first master fundamental abilities before layering on more complex, creative skills, thereby mitigating the trade-offs inherent in trying to optimize for conflicting objectives simultaneously.

# Tackling Reward Hacking with an Adaptive Defense

A well-documented vulnerability in reinforcement learning is “reward hacking,” where an agent learns to exploit its reward function to achieve a high score without genuinely accomplishing the intended goal. In the context of LLMs, this can manifest as generating overly verbose responses to game a length-based reward metric or finding other superficial ways to maximize a score without a true improvement in quality.

To counteract this, they developed and implemented an adaptive defense strategy.

They began by performing an offline analysis of the model’s rollout data to identify recurring patterns of reward-hacking behavior. This empirical analysis informed the creation of a dedicated “Reward Hacking Defense Rubric,” specifically designed to penalize these identified failure modes.
This new rubric was then integrated as a supervisory constraint in all subsequent, more complex RL stages.
The inclusion of this defense mechanism acted as a crucial guardrail, preventing the model from collapsing into reward-hacking states and leading to a marked increase in training stability.

# Impressive Gains on Open-Ended Benchmarks with High Token Efficiency

The Rubicon framework’s efficacy is highlighted by the performance of the Rubicon-preview model, which is a fine-tuned version of the Qwen-30B-A3B. The results are particularly noteworthy for their token efficiency: using a training set of just over 5,000 samples, the model achieved an average absolute improvement of 5.2% across a diverse suite of open-ended and humanity-centric benchmarks. These benchmarks included Creative Writing V3, Writingbench, Judgement V2, and EQ-Bench3, which are designed to evaluate qualities that lack verifiable answers.

Impressively, the 30B-parameter Rubicon-preview model surpassed the performance of the much larger 671B DeepSeek-V3 model by 2.4 percentage points on these subjective tasks.
This achievement underscores the efficiency of the rubric-based method. Furthermore, the specialized training did not lead to a degradation of the model’s core competencies.
Its performance on general ability benchmarks like MMLU was preserved, and it even showed modest improvements on challenging reasoning benchmarks, including a 4.1% gain on AIME 2024 and a 0.8% gain on AIME 2025.

# Stylistic Control and Human-Like Responses

A key advantage of the Rubicon framework is its ability to provide fine-grained control over the stylistic qualities of the LLM’s output.

By using detailed rubrics as explicit “anchors” during training, the model can be guided toward a well-defined and desirable output style. The paper provides examples demonstrating how this approach significantly reduces the generic, “AI-like,” and overly didactic tone that is often characteristic of LLM-generated text.
The resulting responses exhibit greater human-likeness, are more emotionally expressive, and feel less formulaic.
This level of stylistic control is crucial for applications where the quality of the interaction and the user experience are paramount.

# Takeaways for MLEs

The Rubicon paper offers several practical insights for Machine Learning Engineers working on LLM alignment and post-training:

  * **Rubrics as a Viable Reward Signal:** For domains lacking programmatic verifiers (e.g., customer service tone, creative marketing copy), a well-designed rubric system offers a scalable path to applying reinforcement learning. This expands the scope of RL beyond STEM-focused tasks.

  * **Staged Training to Manage Conflicting Objectives:** The “seesaw effect” is a critical consideration. Instead of jointly optimizing for disparate skills like strict instruction-following and open-ended creativity, a staged RL protocol can be more effective. Establishing a baseline of reliable behavior before fine-tuning for more nuanced, creative abilities appears to be a robust strategy.

  * **Iterative, Proactive Defense Against Reward Hacking:** Reward hacking is not just a theoretical risk but an observable failure mode. The key engineering practice here is to treat it as a bug: analyze rollout data, identify how the model is exploiting the reward, and explicitly create a “defense rubric” to penalize that behavior in subsequent training runs. This transforms alignment into an iterative, adaptive process.

  * **High-Signal Data Over Sheer Volume:** The significant performance gains from only 5K training samples highlight a potential new scaling dynamic. For post-training, the combination of high-quality, curated data and a large, diverse set of rubrics can be far more token-efficient than simply scaling up on unfiltered data. This suggests that investment in rubric design and data selection can yield a higher ROI than raw data acquisition.

  * **Stylistic Control is an Engineering Problem:** The ability to reduce “AI-speak” and control for attributes like tone and emotional expressiveness should be treated as a first-class engineering objective, not a qualitative afterthought. Rubrics provide a concrete mechanism to define, measure, and optimize for these stylistic targets.
