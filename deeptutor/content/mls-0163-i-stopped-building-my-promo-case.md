# I stopped building my promo case.

*Machine Learning at Scale collection — Ludovico Bessi, 2026-09-23 · topic: career*

*Then the work started building it for me.*

[](../assets/6582fee223e30183.jpg)

#### How will this read?

For a while I picked projects with one question in mind. How will this read?

Big scope, clever architecture, a good story for the packet. I was so busy building the story that the work suffered.

It’s an easy trap. You take on something oversized to prove you belong at the next level. You go quiet for weeks. Your standup updates get vaguer and less upbeat.

You tell yourself one intense week will close the gap. It never does.

#### Going small

In Ads, mine was a big new ad format. On paper, the perfect project. But that was not how that pan out in the end (story for another time)

When I left YouTube Ads, I went back to my ML roots and made one decision that shaped everything after.

I started picking small problems.

Small problems close. You ship, a number moves or it doesn’t, you learn why, you pick the next one. Days, not quarters.

Two things happened that I didn’t plan for:

  1. The work got fun again.

  2. The anxiety slowly turned into momentum.

#### Let reality answer

Ads had taught me one habit I still lean on: run the experiment and let reality answer. Combine that with small problems and you get feedback all the time.

My confidence stopped coming from how I felt about my work and started coming from results.

#### Outside the OKRs

After enough of these loops, I knew the system well enough to see problems nobody had picked up yet. So I started working outside the OKRs, on ideas I found myself all around the recommendation funnel. And that lead to 17 launches and counting.

Around then I settled into a split I still use.

#### 80/20

Roughly 80% of my time goes to work I can bet on. Problems I understand, a clear path to impact. It keeps me shipping and keeps the team’s trust.

The other 20% goes to things that scare me a bit. Hard, outside what I know, a real chance they go nowhere, a big payoff if they land. That’s where most of my learning happens.

Each half needs the other. The 80% is what lets me afford the 20%.

When a risky bet dies, and some do, I’ve still had a good quarter.

The 20% keeps the 80% from turning into a plateau. Every bet that works eventually becomes something I can count on, and the bar moves up.

The old me wanted all of my work to look like the 20%. Every project was a big bet, so every failure was expensive, so I hedged all of them. It looked ambitious.

But I wasn’t stretching at all in the end!

Just getting more desperate to do the big landing! :(

#### What the work asked for

Finding my own problems, deciding which ones deserve a risky bet, and seeing them through turned out to be most of what the next level asks for.

My biggest 20% bet was large language recommender models. I never set out to learn them. The work kept pulling me there.

The promo-case version of me was trying to look like someone who drives important work. Now I just do a little of it every week. Mostly small. Sometimes scary!

And that seems to be working well enough! (promo or not, i don’t control it so I don’t over stress it)
