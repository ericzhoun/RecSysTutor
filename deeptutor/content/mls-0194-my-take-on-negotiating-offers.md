I believe there are three pillars when it comes to negotiation:

  1. Leverage

  2. Timing

  3. Strategy

… and those are in order of importance!

# Leverage

The most important of it all. You need to know that “you can walk away from it all”.

That means having leverage. Having so many offers that you one telling you to “fuck off” means *nothing* to you. And I mean it.

This can always happen if your current job is just that good ( terms) or you have a lot of good offers.

That’s it.

You have only one offer, and that’s higher comp that your current job (everything else being equal)? then that’s your market rate. you can try. you can win 10k more on TC, but that’s going to be it.

You have other offers, all below your current best offer?

THEN THAT’S YOUR MARKET RATE.

You have other offers, all paying a lot higher than your current job (but with room to grow)?
Now we are talking. however, it’s harder to make them work for you if you lack…

# Timing

Yeah, timing! if your first offer of a gazzilion is exploding today, and you have just finished your last interview for InsertOneFAANGCompany, then… it does not matter?

You can still accept the offer, claim that you are negotiating with future companies, but you made it become messy for no reason. just play with the schedule of interviews and known “HR speed” of a given company to have job offers land all on the same week. then, you have all the time in the world to have them play against each other. (good for you!)

that’s all there is to timing, but it’s crucial.

Now onto the third ingredient.

# Strategy (and where I went wrong)

Let’s say you have multiple offers, all landed in the same week. Congrats, you are playing god mode and people on r/cscareerquestions are fuming because they want to be you.

That was me in 2021. multiple faang offers. now what?

You need to have a goal: rank companies you want to work at from top to bottom, and have companies one up each other all the way to the top.

A few key suggestions:

  1. Prefer email communication over video call. you will only negotiate like this a few times in your life, while for your recruiter it’s a random tuesday. play to your strength (weakness in this case)

  2. Know the market rate + know how well you interviewed == how much you can push

  3. Negotiate everything. but know that from hardest to easiest: salary »»» stock grant »» sign on bonus »» whatever random benefits you like to have, so plan accordingly.

I will tell you what I did wrong and what I did right personally:

  * I was a cocky 24 years old, so I negotiated live with my recruiter. I was able to brute force +XXk USD in stock on my initial stock grant, but man was it brutal

  * I timed my offer perfectly: everything was up to debate in my last week

  * I had a clear target in mind: working at FAANG zurich, my negotiation was structured around that (but i was still OK walking away from it and joining OtherFAANG london, so still had leverage)
