<strong>especially</strong> for Machine learning engineers given the experimental nature of the job.</p><p>Here’s how <em>I am navigating</em> it and how I try to stay ahead of the curve in my big tech job.</p><p>There are many angles in this post, namely:</p><ol><li><p>Design docs are dead.</p></li><li><p>MVPs are your new currency. Less talk, more building.</p></li><li><p>Convincing others that your work matters is easier than you think if you have good taste</p></li><li><p>Relationships matter more and more</p></li></ol><p>Let’s get to it!</p><h2>Design docs are dead</h2><p>Yeah. I said it.</p><p>I have been bearish on design docs as artifacts for a while and I am happy they are mutating into something different.</p><p>I will give you an example from my own experience.</p><p>When I was working in YouTube Ads I worked on a multi-year (!!!) project that was taken and dropped many times due to reorgs.</p><p>Leadership visibility was high, people wanted this shipped badly.</p><p>Looking at the project history, every person coming in spent months writing a spotless doc with multiple alignments.</p><p>The problem?</p><p>The soil under you shifts.</p><p>every.</p><p>single.</p><p>day.</p><p>Infra changes. Top line metrics change.</p><p>Someone somewhere launches something that makes one of your core metrics tank.</p><p>Good luck telling them to un-launch it for your proposal (read this out aloud to realize how stupid this is?)</p><p>If you were to keep your shiny doc up to date with everything changing around you, you’d that full time and never ship ANYTHING.</p><p>If your leadership cares optics and believe this is fine… change orgs.</p><p>What do you do then?</p><p>You start building. Piece by piece. Core functionality first.</p><p>Prove fast that you are moving metrics.</p><p>If you are not… call it a day and move on.</p><p>Your design doc? It just turned into an experimental plan.</p><p>You still have artifacts showing your “design work”.</p><p>Just different flavour of it for the new agentic era.</p><p>Weekly updated in the doc to keep your leadership aware of blockers and what’s happening and what’s your plan.</p><p>Your tech lead can chime in to provide more context and course correct if needed.</p><p>You have a doc to share with your plan to get PRs approved.</p><p>A few new problems with this:</p><ul><li><p>People don’t believe I should work on this initiative at all, don’t see strong grounding for it.</p></li><li><p>But Ludo… people don’t care about my silly MVP, think it’s stupid and don’t approve PRs if the doc is not fully approved</p></li><li><p>But my grand design… how do I prove seniority?</p></li></ul><p>Let’s break them down one by one. ⬇️</p><h2>Convincing others that your work matters is easier than you think if you have good taste</h2><p><em>I write about ML systems in production — the tradeoffs, the architecture decisions, the stuff that doesn’t make it into papers. If you want to go deeper, the paid tier covers the technical (AND personal! like this one) details I can’t fit in free posts.</em></p><div class="paywall-jump" data-component-name="PaywallToDOM"></div><p>You want to do this because you think it’s good idea.</p><p>Why do you think that’s the case? Go to first principles thinking.</p><p>Put yourself in the shoes of your skeptical leads.</p><p>Bad answers:</p><ol><li><p>I feel this is good because I saw this paper from X and want to try this out</p></li><li><p>It’s fun work to pad my CV and kinda related to what we do? pls let me do it</p></li><li><p>We should try this because it worked for this tangentially related use case</p></li></ol><p>See the problem? Vibes. Answer 1 is about you “feeling something”. Answer 2 is around your ego. Answer 3 is a guess.</p><p>If you have worked in the space for 10 years and have PROVEN good taste, then you can make this fly. Because people trust you. (more on this after)</p><p>But before that, you need numbers. COLD. HARD. NUMBERS.</p><p>Good answers:</p><ol><li><p>I see a monetization gap in this user experience. I estimate that by doing X, we will be increasing revenue by Y. We will double check the assumption in 3 weeks with an MVP.</p></li><li><p>I see that “similar team X” did it. There’s no difference between us and them. They obtained results X and Y. Projecting on our numbers I expect to see Z% increase.</p></li><li><p>I already did an MVP in the spare time I created for myself. I see X% increase. I need Y amount of time to cleanup.</p></li></ol><h2>Relationships matter more and more</h2><p>You need trust. Lots of it from different people. We can broadly dive this in new categories</p><p><strong>Your manager</strong></p><p>Your manager must be on board with this way of operating.</p><p>This is surprisingly easy after a few good independent launches that move the needle, even by a small amount.</p><p>Your manager has target goals and as such they are super happy to see you take on work that will drive numbers up, as long as they believe you can bring them all the way to launch.</p><p>Prove it continuously and you will be the golden kid.</p><p><strong>Your tech lead</strong></p><p>Your tech lead must like your style of working. Many tech leads are still on the design doc camp and as such they are a bit more careful around this MVPing around.</p><p>Prove that:</p><ol><li><p>You clean things up after yourself</p></li><li><p>You will achieve impact through good engineering and they will share the wins with you</p></li><li><p>Keep them up to date with your playing around</p></li></ol><p>And you should be golden!</p><p><strong>Your partners across different orgs</strong></p><p>Possibly the hardest sell.</p><p>You come in and expect them to let you submit PRs left and right with comments like “TODO(ludobessi) clean this up after. I know it’s bad but trust me”</p><p>Here, a one pager with a plan goes a long way.</p><p>Also, guard your experiment with flags to make sure you are not breaking ANYTHING.</p><p>Again, trust here goes A LONG WAY.</p><p>In the beginning, be extra careful. After a while, people trust you and auto stamp your PRs, sometimes even with a promise of testing. (Don’t ask me how I know :P)</p><p>Finally: find your people.</p><p>Colleagues with high agency that also approach work in this way. Share the wins with them and together you will thrive.</p><p><strong>Your PM</strong></p><p>… your PM will love you if you operate like this. Trust me.</p><h2>MVPs are your new currency. Less talk, more building.</h2><p>You prove seniority by shipping things. /end.</p><p>And that means a lot of different skills:</p><ol><li><p>Owning the feature end to end</p></li><li><p>Getting buy in from leads to experiment and launch</p></li><li><p>Getting approval from other teams to let you run your experiment on their</p></li><li><p>Getting head count to help you go from MVP to launchable system.</p></li><li><p>… Impact</p></li></ol><p>If you are not rewarded from the above, it’s not you. it’s them. (or it’s you that can’t explain properly your work?)</p><h2>Caveats:</h2><ol><li><p>Documentation is still important. Even more important.</p></li><li><p>Documenting your work remain central to prove you are doing the work. Sorry, that’s just the way the world works.</p></li><li><p>Buy-in and alignment is important. Soft alignment is maybe more important than ever.</p></li></ol>
