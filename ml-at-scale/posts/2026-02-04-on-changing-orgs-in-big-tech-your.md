and out of those 4 years I have changed team…</p><p>three times (!!!!!!)</p><p>You might wonder what goes on in my silly little head, so let’s find out my reasoning behind all the changes, and why I believe it’s a crazy good for your personal growth (at the expense of “on paper” career progression).</p><div class="paywall-jump" data-component-name="PaywallToDOM"></div><p>If you are reading this, you are a paid sub for my newsletter. Thank you so much for being here, I appreciate you supporting me :). Please let me know if you have some topic you’d like me to cover and I will try my hardest to cover it!</p><h1>Changing roles within the company: the why</h1><p>Why even change a team within the same company? Most of the time, changing teams is like a soft-company change:</p><ul><li><p>different people</p></li><li><p>different business problems</p></li><li><p>same tech stack</p></li><li><p>same salary</p></li></ul><p>Many people believe that at that point, you might as well change company and get a nice raise, given you need to “prove yourself again” in the new company either way.</p><p>However, I don’t believe that’s 100% correct.<br>First of all, it’s really location dependent.</p><p>If you are not in the Bay Area, it’s not like there are sooo many tech companies all hiring.</p><p>I live in Zürich, so there’s much lower headcount available at any given time.</p><p>Plus, you need to pass interviews again.</p><p>Plus, you change tech stack.</p><p>I am really in love with Google tech stack, so hard for me to move away from that eheh.</p><p>But, let’s move on to understand why one would want that:</p><ul><li><p>You x10 your learning by completely changing tech stack / business problem</p></li><li><p>You get to know different people / how they work / —&gt; maximize learning from others</p></li><li><p>You get to become an expert in a wide range of things, i.e. you become the person that “has seen that problem already”</p></li></ul><p>Honestly, if I try and think of the person I’d have been without changing teams, I really can’t believe all the learnings I have had on top of that “non-existing” person.</p><p>So… change teams! Move around the company! Look for your own impact. That’s where the fun lies IMHO.</p><h1>A note on career progression and effort</h1><p>If you are moving around a lot, you need to learn a lot of things fast to be up to speed with others. That means that you need to work harder in that ramp up period.</p><p>This means that if you change teams a lot, you end up having to work harder on average IMHO.</p><p>I don’t think that’s a bad thing, but something to be aware of.</p><p>Also, it’s well known that changing teams means “resetting your promo timeline”.</p><p>While I don’t 100% agree with that, given that you can re-use previous artifacts, it’s true that fast advancement would take an hit because of the relationships you need to build, especially at higher levels.</p><h1>Changing roles within the company: the how</h1><p>Lately, moving around teams has become more and more challenging.</p><p>Competition is getting fierce with so many strong candidates both within the company and externally.</p><p>Personally, I have got around ~6 different internal offers, so I know what I am talking about. Here’s my guide:</p><ul><li><p>Be good in your current role. Like really good. With glowing reviews and artifacts that scream “this person does stuff, and does it well”.</p></li><li><p>Write lots of code, don’t be afraid of paper cut fixes that nobody is interested in taking on. I got so many cool projects by just picking up stuff from the floor and diving deeper into problems nobody had the patience to look at. These efforts build up into a snowball if you keep at it.</p></li><li><p>Chat with people! Get to know other teams / their problems. That’s the easiest way to get hired: having someone in the team to vouch for you.</p></li></ul><h1>Changing roles within the company: my personal experience</h1><p>This has been my time at Google:</p><ol><li><p>Joined in March 2022 <strong>Team A </strong></p></li><li><p>Got promoted in <strong>Team A</strong> in November 2023</p></li><li><p>Changed team in May 2024 to <strong>Team B</strong></p></li><li><p>Got Top 2% rating</p></li><li><p>Changed team in June 2025 to <strong>Team C</strong></p></li></ol><p>Team A was about ML Infra / Modelling: 80/20</p><p>Team B was a full stack product SWE role BE / FE: 80/20</p><p>Team C is a Applied AI Product team: full ML modelling</p><p>Different things, lots of learnings and connecting the dots between different orgs is very fun. Would do it again even though I probably lost ~1 year of time that I could have used to get to L5 already. Oh well! :)</p><p>Hope you liked this one :)</p><p>Ludo</p><p></p><p></p><p></p><p></p><p></p>
