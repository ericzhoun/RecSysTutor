crazy.</p><p>On one side: “here’s how linear regression works” tutorials written for people who’ve never touched a dataset. On the other side: academic papers improving some random benchmark by 0.000001% with zero context on why anyone should care.</p><p>Nothing in between. Nothing for the person who’s already working in ML and wants to actually understand how production systems are built and why certain decisions get made.</p><p>So I just started writing about what I was learning in my free time.</p><p>No grand plan. No monetization strategy. Just filling a gap that I personally felt.</p><p>11k newsletter subscribers and 40k LinkedIn followers later… I’m still doing exactly that.</p><p>And I’m genuinely grateful for every single person who reads this! Thank you &lt;3</p><p><strong>How I actually find the time</strong></p><p>I’m not grinding content every evening. That’s not sustainable and it’s not how I operate.</p><p>Here’s the actual system:</p><div class="paywall-jump" data-component-name="PaywallToDOM"></div><p>I save interesting LinkedIn posts, X threads, and papers to a Notion doc as I come across them during the week. No pressure, just collecting.</p><p>Then every once in a while I block 3 hours, sit down, and go through everything I’ve saved. I write in bulk, schedule everything in advance, and then I’m done.</p><p>I log into LinkedIn to comment, then I log off.</p><p>I am not a slave to the platform. The content works for me, not the other way around.</p><p><strong>How I think about content</strong></p><p>I see my content as a funnel.</p><p>At the top: lifestyle, opinions, the occasional shitpost about my life.</p><p>High reach, broad appeal, keeps things human.</p><p>As you go further down: increasingly technical content with real alpha. Things that take time to understand and actually make you better at your job.</p><p>You need both. If you’re only posting memes about ML you’re an entertainer. If you’re only posting dense technical content you’re a textbook.</p><p>The combination is what builds an audience that actually trusts you.</p><p><strong>What building an audience actually feels like</strong></p><p>Here’s something that surprised me: it’s surprisingly “engineerable”.</p><p>Once you have enough followers to get real signal, you start running experiments exactly like you would on an ML system.</p><p>You test different hooks, different formats, different topics. You look at what performs and what doesn’t. You adjust. You iterate.</p><p>The feedback loops are slower than a training run but the process is genuinely similar. Form a hypothesis, test it, read the results, update your priors.</p><p>What’s worked: giving away real value for free. Every piece of content is a trade: I take a few seconds of your attention, and I need to give you something genuinely useful in return.</p><p>That’s the only rule I operate by.</p><p>What flopped: the merch launch. Turns out people are more interested in what I have to say than in a Zürich logo on a hoodie.</p><p>Honestly? I respect it. I’d rather be known for ideas than for streetwear.</p><p><strong>The moment it clicked</strong></p><p>There wasn’t one.</p><p>No single viral post, no overnight explosion, no moment where I looked at the numbers and thought “okay this is real now.”</p><p>Just slow, consistent compounding. And then one day you look up and the growth is starting to accelerate in a way that feels different from before.</p><p>That’s where I am now. And I have no intention of stopping.</p><p>Tomorrow is the last one in this series.</p><p>The bigger picture.</p><p>What I’m actually optimizing for in life right now. Probably the most personal one I’ve written.</p><p>See you then. — Ludo</p><div><hr></div><p>Quick disclaimer before we start: everything I share publicly — in this newsletter, on LinkedIn, anywhere — is either from my own personal experience or based on openly published research and papers. No internal details, no confidential information. I respect my company and I’m not here to be a whistleblower. Just a person sharing what they’re learning.</p><p></p>
