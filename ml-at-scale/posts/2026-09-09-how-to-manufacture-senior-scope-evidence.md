# How to manufacture senior-scope evidence before anyone gives you the title

*Or: the chicken-and-egg buried inside every senior promo*

Nobody is going to hand you senior scope. So how do you get it?

Here is the loop:

Promotion to senior requires evidence that you already operated at senior scope. Scope is assigned by your manager and the org. And the org does not assign senior scope to someone who is not senior yet, because that is a bet nobody is incentivized to place.

You need the scope to earn the level, and you need the level to get the scope.

Most people never see the loop at all :D. They assume promo is a function of working harder on the scope they already have.

**It is not.**

Working harder on your current scope produces a very strong version of your current level, which is exactly the record the committee uses to keep you where you are.

Over the last year I built a senior-scope evidence trail as an L4. No title change. No headcount. No manager redrawing my scope.  
  
Nobody handed me a single thing. Here is the method, in five parts:

  1. Why “do great work and get noticed” is a trap.

  2. Why “ask for bigger scope” is also a trap.

  3. The actual move, which is neither waiting nor asking.

  4. How to build the evidence trail

  5. The failure modes, including the one that will make your own team resent you.

Let me clear the two dead ends first, because most people are stuck in one of them.

**Bad answer #1: Wait to be noticed.**

Do excellent work. Assume the committee will see the quality and reward it.  
They will. They will reward it correctly, as excellent work at your current level. Quality is not scope. A committee looking for senior evidence does not find it in a flawless stack of mid-level deliverables.  
It finds a very reliable mid-level engineer, and it writes exactly that.

**Bad answer #2: Ask for the scope.**

Go to your manager. Request a bigger project, a headcount, a lead role. Two outcomes, both bad. Either you hear “not yet, keep growing,” which quietly resets your clock. Or you get granted something, and granted scope reads in a packet as “was given a chance,” not “created leverage the org came to depend on.”

Assigned scope is a gift, and a gift does not demonstrate the one thing senior is supposed to demonstrate: that you generate scope rather than receive it.

So if you cannot wait for it, and you cannot ask for it, what is left?

There is a third move. It is the one that actually worked for me, and it is the reason I am walking into calibration this year with a packet that argues itself. It has nothing to do with working harder, and nothing to do with lobbying.
