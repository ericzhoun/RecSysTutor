# How to make your work visible to leadership? (as an MLE)

*aka how to thrive in a big company*

[![](../assets/10e55494fdcaaaa1.jpg)](../assets/10e55494fdcaaaa1.jpg)

You do good work:

  * You ship things with impact with good reliability

  * You are easy to work with, always willing to lend an helping hand to everyone around you

  * You come up with new ideas and create opportunities for your team

Yet, somehow, you don’t get the recognition you deserve you should get.

And you chalk it up to “big corp politics”.

The things you tell youself:

  * “I don’t play this game”

  * “My work speaks for itself”

  * “Good work should get recognized. Not my problem”

I used to think like that and let me tell you: this “holier than you” mentality will stall your career FAST.

The problem with this mentality is mainly one: your ego.

And I have a big one! So I know what I am talking about ;)

Let’s see how I shifted things around!

# You are not as important as you think you are (sorry)

Yeah, that’s the cold reality of things.

Your skip is not aware what exactly you are working on.

Sure, maybe at a surface level.

But not the complexity of it.

Not the roadblocks you had to overcome.

Maybe not even the impact.

And a million of other things.

Put yourself in their shoes, they care about much more high level problems.

Once you internalize this, you can then think of a solution.

There are a few things that I believe are important.

## **Sharing your accomplishments the right way**

Share wins, small and big. That does not mean to spam your team chat every time you submit a PR.

But if you are genuine, people will care about your great work. So don’t be shy. Some examples:

  1. Just landed LaunchNumber10. X% impact. I believe it can help your workflow as well because reason A, B and C. Let me know what you think!

  2. Rolling out LaunchNumber15. Heads up to oncall: i expect metrics X and Y to shift. If metric Z moves, please ping me. I will online in the evening to double check the launch.

  3. I have just landed this new shiny thing in prod. It’s not used anywhere yet, but I plan on doing X and Y for reason Z. If you have more ideas on how to use new shiny thing, let me know: happy to experiment with other use cases!

See what I did there?

No fluff corporate texts:

  * Genuine updates

  * Giving heads up

  * Offering a collaboration opportunity to others

Now, this is good, but you can do even better:

  * Ping that Senior Lead with your launch numbers, a nice TLDR of what’s happening and future plans. Keep it short and to the point.

  * Book those blocks with your director to ask questions, drop what you are working and get real-time feedback on your projects / upcoming launches

## **Making your manager’s life easier**

Your manager is being pulled in ten directions. Performance reviews, roadmap planning, headcount battles, skip-level syncs.

The more you can make their job easier, the more visible you become.

Concrete ways to do this:

  * Come to your 1:1 with a clear agenda. Don’t make them ask “so what are you working on?”

  * Send a short weekly update. 3 bullets max. What shipped, what’s blocked, what’s next.

  * When you escalate a problem, come with a proposed solution. Don’t just drop a grenade.

This is being a reliable signal in a noisy environment.

## **Make visibility a habit, not a campaign**

Most people stay invisible for months, then suddenly realize they need to “build their brand” before promo season.

So they start overcommunicating. Posting in every channel. Suddenly very opinionated in meetings.

The fix is simple: make it consistent and low-key.

  * One team update per week. Short.

  * One skip-level touchpoint per month. Casual.

  * One cross-team collaboration surfaced per quarter. Real.

That’s it.

You are not “playing politics” when you share your work.

You are doing your job completely.

Shipping is 80% of the work. The other 20% is making sure the right people understand what you shipped and why it matters.

If you skip that 20%, you are leaving impact on the table. Not just career impact — actual product impact. Because work that isn’t understood doesn’t get funded, extended, or built upon.

So the next time you catch yourself thinking “my work speaks for itself” — ask yourself: _to whom, exactly?_

Good work deserves to be understood. Help people understand it.

That’s not ego!
